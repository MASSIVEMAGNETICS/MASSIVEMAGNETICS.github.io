## 1. Executive Summary

AI AFTER DARK is a proposed AI-native robotic live-entertainment platform. The first commercial format is a 21+ robotic cabaret/nightlife experience using programmable humanoid performers, synchronized music, lighting, character AI, choreography, safety controls, and persistent digital personas.

The company is deliberately **not** starting by buying or leasing a strip club. The launch model is asset-light: partner with an existing properly licensed venue, borrow/rent/demo humanoid hardware from robotics partners, finance production through sponsorship and ticket demand, and use the first event as a measurable proof-of-market.

The long-term company is broader than one venue. The defensible asset is the **Robot Entertainment Operating System (REOS)**: choreography, show control, safety governance, persona orchestration, audience interaction, telemetry, licensing, and event operating procedures that can power robotic concerts, nightlife, branded activations, casinos, conventions, touring shows, and licensed venues.

### Investment thesis

1. Humanoid hardware is becoming materially more accessible. Unitree's official store currently lists the R1 from $4,900, G1 at $13,500, and H2 at $29,900 before integration, logistics, taxes, customization, and safety costs.
2. Robotic nightlife has prior-art demand evidence: Sapphire Las Vegas featured robot pole dancers during CES 2018. That means a generic claim of “America's first robot strip club” is not defensible. The narrower proposed positioning is a **permanent AI-native humanoid robotic adult-entertainment venue**, subject to a formal competitive and trademark review.
3. Existing venues already own the expensive physical infrastructure. Partnering converts fixed cost into negotiated revenue share.
4. The same software, motion library, safety layer, character IP, and telemetry can be reused across many entertainment formats.
5. The startup can validate demand with a single event before institutional capital is required.

## 2. Problem

Nightlife and live entertainment repeatedly depend on expensive physical venues, human scheduling, one-off production, and novelty that decays quickly. Robotics companies, meanwhile, need compelling public demonstrations that show humanoids doing more than laboratory or factory tasks.

AI AFTER DARK connects those problems:

- venues need differentiated traffic and press;
- robotics companies need visible real-world demonstrations;
- audiences seek novel, shareable experiences;
- brands need experiential sponsorship inventory;
- humanoid platforms need recurring applications with measurable revenue.

The concept uses entertainment as the commercialization layer for humanoid robotics.

## 3. Product

### 3.1 Flagship experience

A ticketed 21+ robotic cabaret/show hosted inside an existing licensed venue.

Core elements:

- 1–5 programmable humanoid performers;
- synchronized choreography and music;
- lighting/video-wall/FX orchestration;
- named AI characters with distinct voices and personalities;
- bounded audience interaction;
- tipping/request mechanisms where legally and processor-permitted;
- human operator supervision;
- geofenced stage areas and emergency stops;
- telemetry and show receipts;
- sponsor integrations;
- recorded promotional footage under negotiated rights.

### 3.2 Robot Entertainment Operating System (REOS)

REOS is the intended software/IP moat.

**Modules:**

1. **Persona Engine** — character identity, dialogue, memory boundaries, voice and stage behavior.
2. **Choreography Engine** — approved motion primitives, sequencing, beat synchronization and reusable routines.
3. **Safety Governor** — speed/force limits, stage geofencing, proximity rules, watchdog timers, emergency-stop integration and human override.
4. **Venue Orchestrator** — lighting, audio, displays, cueing and multi-robot scheduling.
5. **Interaction Layer** — bounded audience requests, consent controls and moderation.
6. **Telemetry & Proof Ledger** — uptime, faults, cues, revenue events, operator interventions and post-show performance metrics.
7. **Content/Character Registry** — rights, versions, approved assets and provenance.

### 3.3 Expansion products

- robotic concert performances;
- nightclub/casino robot acts;
- convention and trade-show rentals;
- branded humanoid activations;
- touring robotic entertainment;
- character licensing;
- choreography/motion-pack licensing;
- REOS venue licensing;
- franchise/licensed venue packages;
- digital companion media that does not require physical robots.

## 4. Why Now

Humanoid platforms have moved into price bands where event-scale experimentation is possible without custom industrial-robot budgets. Unitree's current official listings include the R1 from $4,900, G1 at $13,500 and H2 at $29,900. Those advertised prices are **not** complete deployed-system costs: shipping, duties, support, batteries, operator training, insurance, modifications, safety engineering and integration can materially increase total cost.

The strategic opportunity is therefore not “buy robots because they are cheap.” It is to build the reusable entertainment software, operating procedures, show IP and distribution relationships while hardware economics continue improving.

## 7. Market Model — Bottom-Up, Not Hype

Rather than citing a giant third-party “AI + nightlife + robotics” TAM, the company will use an auditable bottom-up model.

### Initial SOM hypothesis

4 metro markets × 2 events/month × 12 months × $15,000 average event gross = **$1.44M annual event gross capacity** before software licensing, rentals, merch or digital revenue.

### Expansion SAM hypothesis

50 viable U.S. metro markets × 4 events/month × 12 months × $15,000 average event gross = **$36M annual event gross capacity** before licensing and other products.

These are capacity scenarios, not market-size facts. They become defensible only after conversion, occupancy, pricing and repeat-attendance data exist.

### TAM policy

Do not publish a top-down TAM until the company can empirically estimate:

- venue density that can legally host the format;
- event frequency before novelty decays;
- repeat-customer rate;
- B2B licensing conversion;
- robot uptime and event capacity;
- average sponsor value.

### Securities pathways to evaluate with counsel

**Regulation D Rule 506(c):** permits broad solicitation, but every purchaser must be an accredited investor and the issuer must take reasonable steps to verify accredited status. The SEC states there is no federal cap on the amount raised under this pathway.

**Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF):** currently permits eligible companies to raise up to $5 million in a 12-month period, including from non-accredited investors within applicable limits. The offering must run through a single SEC-registered broker-dealer or funding portal that is also subject to FINRA requirements. Form C disclosure and ongoing reporting obligations apply.

**Regulation A:** can support larger public raises; SEC guidance currently describes Tier 1 up to $20 million and Tier 2 up to $75 million in a 12-month period, with substantially greater qualification/compliance work.

**Recommended sequence:** sponsor/event revenue first → prototype proof → counsel → select 506(c) for accredited strategic capital or Reg CF if broad public participation is strategically important.

## 10. Entity Architecture

For a venture-scalable structure, evaluate with counsel:

**Parent C-corporation**
- owns REOS software;
- owns brand/IP;
- issues equity;
- signs strategic/licensing agreements.

**Operating subsidiaries / event entities**
- isolate venue/event-specific operational liabilities where appropriate;

A Delaware C-corporation is a common venture-financing structure, but it carries formation, registered-agent, annual-report and franchise-tax obligations. If a Delaware corporation transacts business in Ohio, Ohio foreign-corporation licensing may also apply. Ohio's current Secretary of State fee schedule lists $99 for a foreign for-profit corporation application for license.

This is a structural hypothesis, not legal advice.

## 11. Regulation and Legal Workstreams

### Adult-entertainment classification

Ohio Revised Code regulates sexually oriented businesses and defines, among other things, adult cabarets, specified anatomical areas and operating-hour restrictions. The statutory language includes references to human anatomy, but **the company must not assume that robotic performers fall outside adult-business regulation**. Local zoning and municipal definitions can be more specific.

Before signing a permanent venue lease:

1. obtain written local zoning/classification analysis;
2. determine liquor-license interaction;
3. determine performer/employee rules that might apply;
4. confirm hours and location restrictions;
5. obtain appropriate insurance;
6. confirm fire/building/occupancy compliance;
7. confirm payment-processor/ad-network eligibility.

### Robot safety

Required minimum controls:

- physical audience setback/barriers;
- remote and physical E-stops;
- robot-specific speed/torque limits where available;
- geofenced stage boundaries;
- watchdog shutdown;
- pre-show inspection;
- human operator per defined robot ratio;
- incident logging;
- battery/fire protocol;
- no unsupervised close-contact operation until separately engineered and insured.

### Privacy

Do not build face recognition or persistent audience identity by default. Any camera/microphone functionality should use clear notice, data minimization, retention limits, access controls and explicit opt-in when identity persistence is involved.

### Likeness/IP

Characters must not imitate real performers/celebrities without licensed rights. Maintain provenance for voices, models, costumes, choreography, music and visual assets.

### Design principles

- hardware-agnostic adapters;
- fail-closed safety controls;

- AI may propose choreography, but unreviewed generated motion does not go directly to physical actuators;
- append-only event logs;
- offline-capable show execution;
- explicit operator authority;
- replayable incident telemetry.

## 13. Automation: Capital Engine

The company should operate an automated investor/partner acquisition system with a compliance governor.

### One-button objective

```bash
capital-engine run
```

A run should:

1. discover public investor/partner candidates;
2. collect source evidence;
3. deduplicate;
4. score fit;
5. classify investor type;
6. generate personalized outreach;
7. pass each message through compliance rules;
8. send only messages allowed by the current campaign mode;
9. monitor replies;
10. classify interest;
11. alert the founder only on qualified/high-interest replies;
12. write an auditable receipt for every action.

### Compliance modes

```text
SPONSOR_ONLY
NETWORKING_ONLY
PRE_OFFER_RESEARCH
REG_CF_TEST_WATERS
REG_CF_LIVE
REG_D_506C_LIVE
LEGAL_HOLD
```

The system defaults to `NETWORKING_ONLY`. It must not send investment terms until counsel has approved an offering pathway and the configuration contains an approval record/hash.

### Prospect score

100-point deterministic baseline:

| Signal | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Thesis fit: robotics/AI/entertainment/hospitality | 25 |
| Stage fit | 20 |
| Typical check-size fit | 15 |
| Portfolio adjacency | 15 |
| Geography | 10 |
| Recent investment/activity | 10 |
| Verified contact channel | 5 |

No contact information may be invented. Every prospect record requires a source URL and retrieval timestamp.

### CRM state machine

```text
DISCOVERED
  -> VERIFIED
  -> SCORED
  -> QUEUED
  -> SENT
  -> REPLIED
  -> INTERESTED
  -> QUALIFIED
  -> MEETING
  -> DILIGENCE
  -> COMMITTED

Terminal/side states:
SUPPRESSED / UNSUBSCRIBED / BOUNCED / NOT_A_FIT / LEGAL_HOLD
```

### Local-first data model

SQLite tables:

- `prospects`
- `prospect_evidence`
- `campaigns`
- `messages`
- `replies`
- `suppression_list`
- `compliance_events`
- `meetings`
- `receipts`

### Outreach rules

- start at low daily volume to protect domain/mail reputation;
- accurate sender identity and subject lines;
- physical postal address where legally required;
- functional unsubscribe mechanism;
- permanent suppression list;
- do not scrape or message sources in violation of their terms;
- no deceptive claims, fake traction, fake investor names or fake scarcity;
- actual securities terms only in the legally enabled offering mode;
- if email content itself becomes sexually explicit, evaluate the FTC Adult Labeling Rule before sending.

### Interest alert

A reply is flagged when it contains strong evidence of an investment/partnership next step, for example:

- requests a deck/data room;
- asks for meeting times;
- asks valuation/terms/check-size questions;
- asks for financials, cap table or diligence;
- offers venue/robot/sponsor resources;
- explicitly states investment interest.

The system sends the founder a notification containing the prospect, source, conversation summary, interest score, requested next action, and recommended response.
