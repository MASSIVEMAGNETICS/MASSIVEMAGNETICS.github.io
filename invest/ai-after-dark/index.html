The thesis

Do not finance a building to prove an experience. Prove the experience using other people's existing assets, convert that proof into proprietary software/data/IP, then finance the scalable layer.

Humanoid hardware has entered price bands where event-scale experiments are possible: Unitree's official store currently lists R1 from $4,900, G1 at $13,500 and H2 at $29,900 before shipping, taxes, support, customization, insurance and integration. Hardware cost is no longer the only barrier; dependable choreography, safety, operations, distribution and monetization are the real system.

Not just a “robot strip club”

Flagship

A ticketed, visually intense, adult-nightlife robotic show inside a properly licensed partner venue: humanoids, synchronized music, lighting, named AI characters, bounded interaction and human-supervised motion.

Platform

The same system can power robot concerts, casinos, conventions, branded activations, touring shows, character licensing and licensed third-party venues.

Prior art matters: Sapphire Las Vegas used robotic pole dancers during CES 2018. Therefore the company will not claim the broad title “America's first robot strip club.” A narrower “first permanent AI-native humanoid robotic adult-entertainment venue” position remains a working hypothesis pending competitive/legal verification.

Public capital is a legal system, not a button

Rule 506(c)

Can support broad solicitation, but purchasers must be accredited and accredited status must be reasonably verified. No federal offering cap under the rule.

Regulation CF

Can include non-accredited investors within applicable limits and currently supports up to $5M in 12 months, but transactions must occur through one registered intermediary and Form C/ongoing obligations apply.

Regulation A

Potential later-stage public pathway: SEC guidance describes up to $20M Tier 1 and $75M Tier 2 in 12 months, with substantially more qualification and compliance work.

The intended sequence is event/sponsor proof first, then securities counsel chooses the actual financing path. The website will not silently morph into an illegal stock checkout page.

Hard risks

Regulatory / payments

Adult-business classification, local zoning, liquor-license interaction, operating hours, insurance, payment processors, advertising rules and securities law can all block execution. Written legal/classification work comes before a permanent lease or public investment checkout.

Physical safety / reliability

Humanoids are powerful machines. Production requires audience separation, E-stops, geofencing, operator supervision, battery/fire protocols, inspections and fail-closed show control. Novelty is worthless if uptime sucks or somebody gets hurt.

Brand / ethics / privacy

The company will position the platform as a new physical entertainment medium, not as “replacing women.” No unlicensed celebrity likenesses, no default facial recognition, and no silent biometric memory.

Novelty decay

If customers attend once and never return, the venue thesis fails. Character development, rotating shows, touring and B2B licensing are designed to create repeatable value beyond the stunt.

Kill criteria

  • customers will not pay enough to support event contribution margin;
  • robot uptime/reliability cannot support live shows;
  • regulatory, insurance or venue economics destroy the model;
  • repeat attendance collapses after novelty;
  • venues/sponsors view the format as materially brand-negative;
  • REOS cannot create reusable value beyond one viral event.
