# 1. The Problem: A Precise Accounting Gap

Modern institutions measure financial flows with extraordinary precision. Companies track revenue, margins, utilization, conversion, throughput, retention, and productivity. Platforms measure clicks, sessions, dwell time, impressions, and engagement. AI systems are benchmarked on capability, accuracy, latency, token cost, task completion, and increasingly productivity impact. Time appears throughout these systems, but usually as an input, duration, or constraint - not as a resource whose ownership and final disposition are audited.

This matters because a reduction in required labor does not automatically create an equivalent increase in human freedom. A tool may reduce a task from eight hours to six hours while the two-hour difference is reassigned to additional mandatory work. A platform may make an activity easier while using behavioral optimization to increase total time spent. An automation may remove repetitive steps while creating new monitoring, debugging, coordination, or vendor-lock-in costs. Conventional productivity statistics can correctly report improvement while saying little about whether the affected person's discretionary lifetime increased.

The proposed accounting gap is therefore not 'economists forgot time.' They did not. The gap is narrower: the disposition, ownership, and agency quality of temporal surplus are rarely treated as first-class auditable variables across technology, organizations, and digital systems.

> **Time saved ≠ Time returned.** A productivity gain can exist without an equivalent gain in temporal sovereignty.

*Figure 1. The disposition problem: technical time savings can be returned, recaptured, or consumed by new friction.*

## 1.1 Why the distinction matters now

The distinction becomes more important as AI expands the amount of work that can be produced per unit of direct human effort. Field and experimental studies already show material productivity improvements from generative AI in specific tasks, while survey evidence reports measurable time savings. [7]-[9] The policy and design question is what happens to the surplus.

- If surplus time is voluntarily reclaimed by the person, automation can increase temporal sovereignty.
- If surplus time is automatically converted into additional required output, productivity rises while sovereign time may not.
- If surplus time is replaced by tool supervision, debugging, or fragmented attention, nominal savings may be overstated.
- If a platform optimizes for continued engagement rather than user benefit, increased time-on-system may represent either valued experience or extraction; duration alone cannot distinguish them.

# 2. Prior Art and Novelty Boundary

A credible framework must begin by stating what is not new. Human time has a long intellectual history across economics, psychology, sociology, labor studies, development, and human-computer interaction.

| Tradition | Established contribution | What remains open for this paper |
|---|---|---|
| Time-allocation economics | Becker formalized household allocation of time among market and non-market activities. [1] | Track control and disposition of technology-created temporal surplus. |
| National Time Accounting | Krueger, Kahneman, Schkade, Schwarz, and Stone linked time use to experienced well-being. [2] | Add system-level surplus ownership, maintenance, and agency variables. |
| Time-use statistics | BLS and OECD measure how populations allocate daily time. [3][4] | Instrument technology-mediated transitions and counterfactual time saved/returned. |
| Time poverty / time-use agency | Development research measures excessive demands on time and, increasingly, flexibility to reallocate it. [5] | Generalize agency-adjusted time return to AI, software, workplaces, and platforms. |
| Working-time research | ILO research connects working hours, work-life balance, well-being, health, and productivity. [6] | Measure temporal surplus after automation rather than only scheduled hours. |
| Attention scarcity | Simon and later attention-economy work treat attention as scarce in information-rich environments. [13] | Anchor attention as a downstream claim on finite lifetime and incorporate stopability/ownership. |
| AI productivity | Experiments and field studies estimate output gains and time savings from AI. [7]-[9] | Measure who controls the savings and net costs after rework and maintenance. |
| Dark-pattern research | Regulators study interfaces that impair consent or steer users through deceptive design. [10] | Connect agency-preserving design to lifetime accounting rather than engagement alone. |

> **NOVELTY CLAIM — DELIBERATELY NARROW:** The proposed novelty is the synthesis and operationalization of a cross-domain accounting layer in which (1) time saved and time returned are separated, (2) human control over the surplus is explicitly scored, and (3) maintenance, rework, fragmentation, and future obligations are booked as temporal liabilities.

# 3. First Principles: Human Lifetime as a Bounded Resource

A human lifetime differs from ordinary financial capital. Remaining duration is finite but uncertain, cannot be stockpiled for later use, expires continuously, and is only partially transferable through delegation. These properties make clock time an unusually constrained substrate for all downstream activity.

> **Σᵢ Tᵢ ≤ L** — all activities compete for a bounded remaining lifetime L.

*Figure 2. A proposed resource hierarchy. Attention, labor, and monetary production are downstream of finite lifetime.*

## 3.1 Clock time is not enough

One hour of uninterrupted self-directed work is not functionally equivalent to one hour fragmented by interruptions, coercion, exhaustion, or recovery. The framework therefore distinguishes chronological duration from effective sovereign time.

> **EHT = ∫ q(t) · a(t) · c(t) dt**  
> EHT: Effective Human Time. q = functional quality/capacity; a = agency/self-direction; c = continuity/usable concentration. Each term is normalized to [0,1].

This is a conceptual measure, not a validated psychometric scale. Its purpose is to prevent a false equivalence between all minutes. Any empirical version would require validated instruments, subgroup analysis, and sensitivity testing.
