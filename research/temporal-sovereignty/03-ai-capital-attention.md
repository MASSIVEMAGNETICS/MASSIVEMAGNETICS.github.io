# 6. AI, Automation, and the Disposition of Time Surplus

Generative AI creates a clean empirical setting because the technology can reduce task completion time while leaving open the institutional question of who receives the benefit. Noy and Zhang found productivity improvements in professional writing tasks. [7] Brynjolfsson, Li, and Raymond reported roughly 14% productivity improvement for customer-support agents using AI assistance. [8] Bick and coauthors reported that users perceived time savings equivalent to a measurable share of total work hours. [9]

> **Productivity gain → temporal surplus → disposition**  
> The middle term exists even when conventional accounting stops at productivity.

## 6.1 Four surplus regimes

| Regime | Example | Productivity | STR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Returned surplus | Task takes 2 hours less and worker may use the 2 hours freely | Up | High |
| Recaptured surplus | Task takes 2 hours less; quota rises by 2 hours equivalent | Up | Low/zero |
| Maintenance substitution | Manual work falls, but supervision/debugging replaces much of it | May rise | Low/variable |
| Capability expansion | Same time produces substantially more chosen output for the user | Up | Potentially high |

This framework does not prescribe that every productivity gain must become leisure. People may voluntarily choose more output, income, learning, caregiving, or creation. Temporal sovereignty concerns meaningful control over the allocation, not mandatory idleness.

# 7. Temporal Capital and Temporal Debt

Temporal capital is any durable asset created today that reduces expected future human effort or increases future capability without proportionally increasing human maintenance. Examples include reliable automation, reusable knowledge, documentation, training, software libraries, reputation, catalogs, trusted data, and institutional memory.

> **Temporal capital: present human effort → repeated future reduction in required human effort.**  
> The asset compounds when reuse is reliable and maintenance remains bounded.

Temporal debt is the inverse: a current choice that creates expected future claims on human time. Technical debt is one special case. Other cases include duplicated projects, brittle integrations, recurring manual workflows, undocumented decisions, subscription sprawl, unresolved operational obligations, and context that must be reconstructed repeatedly.

> **OPERATIONAL IMPLICATION:** An unfinished project is not only an incomplete asset. It can become a creditor against future attention. Closure, archival, and deliberate abandonment can therefore be legitimate temporal-debt reduction strategies.

# 8. Attention, Interfaces, and Agency

Attention is treated here as a downstream allocation mechanism for lifetime, not as a synonym for lifetime itself. A person can be physically present but cognitively fragmented; conversely, a highly absorbing activity can be intentionally chosen and valuable. Ethical temporal accounting therefore requires agency-sensitive measurement.

The Applied Human Attention Dynamics (AHAD) framework developed within the same research program already proposes multidimensional evaluation of comprehension, memory, semantic fidelity, fatigue, safety, stopability, transparency, alternatives, and ownership rather than using completion or engagement as sufficient success metrics. [11] That framework can serve as the human-interface measurement layer for temporal sovereignty.

> **Agency-preserving temporal score = STR × VAPI**  
> VAPI = a validated future version of a voluntary-agency preservation index; current AHAD formulation is provisional.

## 8.1 Dual-use warning

The same predictive systems that can reduce search and improve comprehension can also optimize capture. Semantic personalization, recommendation, persuasive design, and adaptive interfaces are dual-use. The objective function and governance layer therefore matter at least as much as raw capability.
